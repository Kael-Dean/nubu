# Arm & Hand Reference: Flat Vector Cut-out Puppet (muscular adult male, front-facing)

Scope: arms and hands only. Semi-realistic flat illustration, rigged with bones and image/vector pieces
(Rive is primary; Spine/Moho/Toon Boom notes included). This doc answers three faults the owner rejected:

- (a) Palm-forward vs back-of-hand gestures all reuse one supinated forearm drawing.
- (b) In the double-biceps flex, the forearm looks detached at the elbow.
- (c) Relaxed arms read as different lengths and thicknesses.

Sources are listed at the end. Numbers marked *est.* are working values for drawing. They come from
canon proportions plus typical muscular-male measurements, so check them against photo reference.

---

## 0. The one rule that fixes fault (a)

**Forearm rotation is measured against the elbow, not against the camera.**

The ulna hinges on the humerus and does not roll. The radius rolls over it and carries the hand
([Proko, arm bones](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-arm-bones-anatomy-for-artists/notes)).
So the **elbow end** of the forearm always shows whatever surface the upper arm dictates. The **wrist end**
shows whatever surface the hand shows. To classify any pose, compare the two ends:

| Elbow end shows... | Wrist end shows... | Bones | Forearm state | Shape to draw |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same surface as the hand (e.g. crook + palm) | palm side | parallel | **supinated** | straight, wide, flat-fronted wedge |
| Surface 90° off from the hand | thumb edge or pinky edge | half-crossed | **neutral** | narrower, the brachioradialis forms a ridge |
| Opposite surface to the hand (e.g. crook + back of hand) | opposite | **crossed (X)** | **pronated** | rounder tube with a visible spiral twist |

Consequence, and the trap our rig fell into: "back of hand to camera = pronated" is only true when the
arm **hangs down**. When the elbow points down and the forearm points up (peace sign, oath pose), the
**palm-to-camera** hand is the pronated one. The camera sees the back of the forearm at the elbow end and
the palm at the wrist end, so the ends are opposite and the bones cross. Always run the table above.
Never infer the forearm drawing from the hand alone.

## 1. Pronation and supination, seen from the front

### 1.1 Bones
- **Radius**: thumb side at the wrist, lateral at the elbow. It is thin at the elbow and wide at the wrist.
  It carries the hand. Its lower third is boxy, which is why the wrist reads as a box ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-arm-bones-anatomy-for-artists/notes)).
- **Ulna**: pinky side at the wrist, medial at the elbow. It is thick at the elbow, forming the **olecranon**,
  and thin at the wrist, forming the **ulnar head bump**. It barely moves.
- **Supination** (palm up or forward): the bones are parallel. **Pronation** (palm down or back): the radius
  crosses over the ulna in an X ([Kenhub](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/pronation-and-supination); [Radiopaedia](https://images.radiopaedia.org/articles/movements-of-the-forearm?lang=us)).
- Pronator teres runs diagonally from the inner elbow toward the thumb side. It is the visible "diagonal"
  in a pronated forearm ([Bridgman-era text, chestofbooks](https://chestofbooks.com/arts/drawing/Anatomy/The-Forearm-Anatomy-And-Movements-Masses-The-Arm-Anatomy.html)).

### 1.2 Muscle masses that make the silhouette
- **Extensor-supinator mass** (brachioradialis plus long wrist extensors). It starts on the **outer**
  humerus about a third of the way up the upper arm, so it **overlaps into the upper arm** as a wedge. It
  peaks just below the elbow and becomes tendon halfway down the forearm ([chestofbooks](https://chestofbooks.com/arts/drawing/Anatomy/The-Forearm-Anatomy-And-Movements-Masses-The-Arm-Anatomy.html)).
  It always follows the thumb.
- **Flexor-pronator mass**. It starts on the **inner** epicondyle, sits lower, and tapers to the wrist.
- **Ulnar furrow**. Where the two masses meet on the back of the forearm, the ulna shows as a groove or
  ridge running from the olecranon to the pinky-side wrist bump. Bridgman describes it as a thin dagger
  pointing toward the little finger.
- If you skip the brachioradialis wedge that tucks into the upper arm, the arm reads as "a pipe fitting"
  ([Creative Bloq, drawing arms](https://creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-an-arm)). This is the root of fault (b).

### 1.3 Silhouette change, arm hanging, viewed from the front

| | Supinated (A-pose, palms forward) | Neutral (relaxed, thumbs forward) | Pronated (back of hand forward) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall forearm shape | **Widest** across the front, flat face, clear taper to the wrist | medium width; the front contour is the brachioradialis ridge | **rounder**, tube-like ("egg or tube", [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-an-arm)) with a spiral |
| Brachioradialis bulge | **outer** contour, high, just below the elbow | faces the camera, forming the front ridge; shows on the outer contour near the elbow | wraps from outer at the elbow toward the **front-inner** side lower down, giving an S-shaped spiral |
| Flexor mass | **inner** contour, lower bulge | inner contour | mostly hidden behind; only a little shows on the outer contour |
| Ulna line | hidden (on the back) | partly hidden; the inner edge runs straight | **visible** as a furrow down the back of the forearm to a **pinky-side wrist knob** |
| Wrist width as seen | **wide** (the broad dimension of the oval, about 1.5x its depth) | **narrow** (you see its depth) | wide, with the ulnar head bump on the pinky side |
| Thumb | points **outward** (away from body) | points **forward** at the camera, foreshortened | points **inward** (toward thigh) |
| Hand shows | palm | thumb edge, index knuckle, narrow hand | back of hand, knuckles |
| Elbow seen | **soft crook** (cubital fossa) facing camera; olecranon hidden behind | crook faces forward-inward; olecranon sits at back-outer | crook usually turns **inward** because the shoulder also rotates in; the **olecranon point** shows on the outer contour |
| Carrying angle | visible: forearm angles **outward** about 10–15° from the upper arm | mostly hidden | hidden |

### 1.4 Upper arm and shoulder rotation
A real arm rarely pronates the forearm alone. People add humerus rotation, and that changes the upper
arm drawing too:

| Shoulder rotation | Upper arm facing camera | Elbow | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **External** (rolled out) | biceps full front; deltoid front head | crook faces the camera | A-pose palms forward |
| **Neutral** | biceps front-inner; deltoid side and front; triceps lateral head on the outer contour | crook forward-inward | relaxed stand |
| **Internal** (rolled in) | **outer** arm: deltoid side, triceps lateral head, brachialis; biceps shifts to the inner contour | crook faces the body; **olecranon points out or back** | hands behind, back of hand forward, finger heart at chest |
| **Abducted + external** (arm up and out, forearm up) | **inner** arm: biceps on the **top** contour, triceps hanging **below**, armpit groove facing the camera | crook faces up and in; **olecranon at the lower-outer corner** | wave, double-biceps, surrender |

Landmarks: the elbow points (two epicondyles plus the olecranon) line up **horizontally** when the arm is straight. They form a
**triangle** with the olecranon dropping lower when the arm is bent ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-arm-bones-anatomy-for-artists/notes)).
The inner epicondyle is the larger, more visible knob.

## 2. Per-gesture table (front-facing character)

Piece names are proposed. Each forearm comes in `_L`/`_R`, but **draw `_R` and mirror it** (see section 4).

| # | Gesture | Hand side to camera | Forearm rotation (vs elbow) | Elbow points | Upper arm visible side | Pieces needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Relaxed stand**, arms at sides, thumbs forward, hands toward thighs | thumb edge + index knuckle (radial side) | **neutral** | back / slightly out, about 10–15° of bend | front-outer: deltoid side, biceps front, triceps hint outside | `upperarm_front`, `forearm_neutral`, `hand_side_relaxed` |
| 2 | **A-pose, palms forward** | palm | **supinated** | down and back; crook faces camera; carrying angle shows | full front (external rotation) | `upperarm_front`, `forearm_supine`, `hand_palm_open` |
| 3 | **Waving hi beside head** (upper arm out about 45–90°, forearm up) | palm, thumb toward head | **neutral** relative to the elbow, because the shoulder supplies the external rotation | **down-out** | **inner arm**: biceps on top, triceps below | `upperarm_inner_raised`, `forearm_neutral_raised` (shows the ulnar or inner side at the elbow end), `hand_palm_open`, plus `elbow_cap_bent` |
| 4 | **Peace sign at shoulder, palm out** (K-pop/selfie; elbow down by the ribs, forearm up) | palm, fingers up | **pronated** (crossed: back of forearm at the elbow end, palm at the wrist end) | **down** | front (biceps), foreshortened and short | `upperarm_front_short`, `forearm_prone_up` (ulna furrow and olecranon at the bottom), `hand_V_palm`, `elbow_cap_bent` |
| 5 | **Thumbs up at chest** (forearm across the chest, knuckles forward) | back of fingers / knuckles, thumb up | **neutral** (thumb-up is always neutral) | **down-out** | outer arm (internal rotation): deltoid side, triceps | `upperarm_outer`, `forearm_neutral_across` (brachioradialis ridge on the top contour), `hand_fist_thumbup_dorsal` |
| 6 | **Pointing up, index beside head** | common: palm at 3/4, thumb toward head. Variant: back of hand | common: neutral → slightly pronated; back-of-hand variant: **supinated** | **down-out** | inner-front, depending on how high the elbow is | `upperarm_inner_raised` or `upperarm_front_short`, `forearm_neutral_raised` / `forearm_supine_raised`, `hand_point_up_palm` / `_dorsal` |
| 7 | **Double-biceps flex** (upper arm about horizontal, slightly above shoulder and 10–20° forward; forearm vertical; fists curled toward the head) | pinky edge of the fist + curled fingers; palms toward the face | **supinated**, to peak the biceps ([StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/bodybuilding-poses/); [Bodybuilding.com](https://bodybuilding.com/fun/posing-like-a-pro-video-3-mandatory-poses-from-front)) | **down-out**, olecranon at the lower-outer corner | **inner arm**: biceps peak on top, triceps hanging below | **pre-bent single drawing** `arm_flex_bent` (upper arm, elbow, and forearm in one piece), or `upperarm_flex` + `forearm_supine_up` + `elbow_flex_cap`. Hand: `hand_fist_flex` |
| 8 | **Korean finger heart at chest** (thumb crossed over the index, other fingers curled) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_heart)) | thumb-index cross on top; back of hand at 3/4 | **neutral → slightly pronated** | **down-out** | outer-front (mild internal rotation) | `upperarm_front`, `forearm_neutral_up` (short, diagonal up and in), `hand_fingerheart_dorsal34` |
| 9 | **Holding a mic to the mouth** (fist around a vertical mic below the chin) | knuckles / back of hand at 3/4; thumb on top toward the mouth | **neutral** (palm toward the midline or mouth) | **down**, slightly out and forward | front-outer | `upperarm_front_short`, `forearm_neutral_up`, `hand_fist_mic` (mic as a separate piece between the fingers and the thumb) |

Notes:
- The palm-out V is the peace or victory sign. Palm-in is an insult in the UK, Ireland, Australia and
  New Zealand ([Wikipedia, V sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_sign)). Keep the palm facing out.
- Gestures 3, 4 and 6 all have the hand beside the head with the palm out, but they need **different
  forearms**. Gesture 4 (elbow down) is pronated. Gesture 3 (elbow out) is neutral. The difference comes
  from where the elbow is. Check this first.
- Minimum forearm set for these 9 gestures: `forearm_supine`, `forearm_neutral` (hanging and raised
  variants), `forearm_prone_up`, `forearm_neutral_across`, plus one **pre-bent flex arm**. Each one is
  drawn once for the right arm and mirrored.

Photo reference to pull (search terms that return clean front views): "front double biceps pose",
"surrender pose hands up reference", "oath hand raised reference", "kpop peace sign selfie", "finger heart
hand close up", "singer holding mic front view". [Dimensions.com](https://www.dimensions.com/element/front-double-biceps-pose)
has a measured front double-biceps diagram.

## 3. Joint construction: elbows and shoulders

### 3.1 Techniques and what they're good for

| Technique | How | Strength | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Round joint caps (classical articulation)** | End each segment in a **circle** centred on the pivot. The top piece's outline is trimmed, so only its fill crosses the joint ([Toon Boom, fix articulations](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-12-2/premium/rigging/fix-articulations.html)) | simple; no seam at any angle up to about 60–90° | past about 90° a gap or notch appears. Toon Boom suggests extra fold lines on an overlay for bent elbows. The ball shape looks puppet-like on a realistic arm |
| **Patch / extra elbow piece** | A third piece (elbow skin, fill only) drawn **under both segments**, or over them as a patch parented to one segment ([Toon Boom patch articulation](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-12-2/premium/rigging/patch-articulation.html)) | fills the crook and the olecranon corner | has to be counter-rotated or swapped at large angles; flat colour only |
| **Mesh + weights (Rive, Spine)** | One continuous mesh across the elbow. Vertices near the joint get weights blended between the two bones ([Rive bones](https://rive.app/docs/editor/manipulating-shapes/bones)) | the pattern and outline stay continuous; best at small and medium bends | **collapses (loses volume) around 90°** and looks bad beyond that ([Spine forum](https://de.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/28431-questions-on-best-practices--most-efficient-methods-for-character-animations)); a texture or tattoo smears on the inner crook |
| **Extra joint or helper bone** | A short bone at the elbow, weighted to the vertices that collapse ([Spine forum](https://ja.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/7673-best-practices-for-rigging-mesh-joints)). Moho uses an "elbow bone" to keep volume ([Moho tips, Monash](https://users.monash.edu.au/~myless/catnap/mohotips/limbs.html)) | keeps the outer elbow round | needs keying or a constraint to follow the bend |
| **Corrective shape (Moho smart bone / Spine deform key / Rive solo or blend state)** | The elbow-bend angle drives a correction: the crease goes in on the inside, a rounded olecranon on the outside, biceps bunching ([Lost Marble forum](https://www.lostmarble.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=171792); [smart actions](https://www.animestudiotutor.com/bones/smart_bone_actions_in_moho_anime_studio/)) | best realism inside a deform rig | authoring time; only one correction per angle range |
| **Drawing swap (pre-bent arm)** | Switch to a pre-drawn bent arm at extreme poses. Spine stores these as a "setup pose animation" and binds new art to the existing bones ([Spine blog](https://us.esotericsoftware.com/blog/Rigging-new-poses-tutorial)) | anatomically correct at the hero angle; tattoo drawn continuous | pops if you swap mid-motion; tattoo art must be redrawn per drawing |

### 3.2 Recommendation for this character
For a semi-realistic muscular arm with a full tattoo sleeve on one arm, where the pattern must stay continuous:

1. **Split by gesture family, not by angle.** Keep **one deformable arm per forearm-rotation family**:
   supine, neutral, and prone. Each is a single continuous mesh running shoulder → elbow → wrist, so the
   tattoo is painted once across the elbow and never cut by a seam. Use it for bends **between 0° and about 70°**.
2. **For bends of 90° and more (flex, peace sign, mic), swap in a pre-bent whole-arm drawing.** Draw the
   biceps bunch, the forearm flesh pressing into the biceps, and the olecranon corner by hand. Paint the
   tattoo **on the bent drawing itself**, following the folded surface, so it stays continuous there too.
   Add a small mesh to that drawing so it can still flex about ±15° for life.
3. **Hide the swap**: change the drawing on a held frame or at the moment of a fast motion (anticipation
   or smear). Match bone positions so the shoulder and wrist land on the same pixels. Spine docs warn that
   attachment keys at runtime can fire before the bones settle, so set the swap one frame later.
4. **Under-piece safety net**: keep an `elbow_under` fill shape (skin colour, no outline, no tattoo) under
   both segments of every deformable arm. If weights collapse for a frame, skin shows instead of a gap.
5. **Shoulder**: the upper arm goes **under** the deltoid. Draw the deltoid as its own cap, parented to
   the torso and rotating partly with the upper arm (about 50%, via a constraint), so the shoulder never
   shows a notch. The deltoid inserts on the **outer** upper arm about halfway down, so its tip must ride
   on the upper-arm piece.
6. **Never** reuse a forearm from another rotation family by flipping or skewing it. A pronated forearm has
   a different contour (spiral, ulna furrow), not a mirrored one.

### 3.3 Fixing fault (b): forearm detached in the flex
Why it fails: two capsule shapes meet at a single pivot. A real bent elbow is **wider than either segment**,
and the elbow corner "becomes two corners" in a tight bend ([Scribd, arms & hands](https://www.scribd.com/document/661694119/09-The-Arms-and-Hands)).
Build it like this:
- **Elbow corner**: shoulder, elbow, and wrist must **not** line up. Draw a clear downward triangle or
  square corner at the olecranon, or the arm "reads as broken or rubbery" ([edge.airmedia, how to draw an elbow](https://edge.airmedia.org/draw-elbow)).
- **Inside**: the forearm's flexor mass and the brachioradialis **press into and overlap the biceps**. There
  is no negative space between them; the crease is a single dark line, not a gap.
- **Outside, lower contour**: the triceps flows **into** the olecranon. Stop the triceps line about
  0.1 H before the corner and let the forearm outline continue it.
- **Pivot placement**: put the elbow bone joint at the **centre of the elbow mass**, slightly toward the
  outer (olecranon) side, not at the inner crease. Spine and Mixamo users report that moving the joint
  toward the outer skin stops flattening ([Unity forum](https://forum.unity3d.com/threads/joints-flattening-in-mixamo.472904/)).
- **Forearm root width** must be at least the elbow width (about 0.40–0.45 H), not the forearm-belly width
  of the hanging arm.
- If it still separates: use the pre-bent swap (3.2-2).

## 4. Proportions

H = head height (chin to crown). Figure about 7.5–8 H.

### 4.1 Lengths
| Segment | In head heights | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder (acromion) → elbow (epicondyle) | **about 1.5 H** | elbow at the bottom of the rib cage / natural waist when hanging ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-arm-bones-anatomy-for-artists/notes); [fiveable](https://fiveable.me/drawing-foundations/unit-10/proportions-human-body/study-guide/bVYcw96jRRpCUZJk)) |
| Elbow → wrist | **about 1.15 H** | wrist at the crotch / greater-trochanter level |
| Wrist → middle fingertip | **about 0.8 H** (about the face length, chin to hairline) | fingertips slightly **below mid-thigh** ([Heidelberg, Oehler](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/oehler1927/0056)) |
| Armpit → fingertip | **3 H** | Richer canon ([Proko proportions](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-average-figure/notes)) |
| Acromion → epicondyle = epicondyle → knuckles | equal | Richer check, fastest way to catch fault (c) |
| Segment ratios vs stature (biomechanics) | upper arm ≈ 0.186, forearm ≈ 0.146, hand ≈ 0.108 of height | widely reproduced Drillis-Contini/Winter constants ([PSU OpenLab](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/category/tools)); at 8 H this gives 1.49 / 1.17 / 0.86 H |

Shoulder width (male) is about 2 H, acromion to acromion, outside the deltoids measuring a little more.

### 4.2 Widths for a muscular build (front view, arm hanging, neutral) *est.*
| Level | Width | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Deltoid (cap, outer edge → armpit line) | 0.65–0.75 H | widest point of the arm; sits outside the torso line |
| Biceps / upper arm belly | 0.55–0.60 H | about 40 cm circumference on a muscular arm |
| Just above the elbow | 0.42–0.45 H | the arm narrows; the brachioradialis wedge starts here on the outside |
| Elbow (epicondyles) | 0.38–0.42 H | bony and slightly squared |
| Forearm belly (just below elbow) | 0.45 H supine / 0.38 H neutral / 0.42 H prone | **wider than the elbow** when supinated: the classic "forearm bulge" |
| Wrist | 0.27 H wide side / 0.18 H depth | neutral relaxed hands show the **narrow** value |
| Hand at knuckles | 0.38 H palm/back view / 0.15–0.2 H edge view | |

The rhythm runs wide (deltoid) → narrow (above elbow) → wide (forearm belly, high on the outside) →
narrow (wrist). Avoid parallel tubes ([Proko forearms](https://www.proko.com/s/Nsfi)).
Contours alternate: the outer bulge peaks high and the inner bulge peaks lower (the "offset" rule).

### 4.3 Making two mirrored arms read identical (fault c)
1. **Draw one arm and mirror it** (scaleX = −1 on a parent group). Never draw the left and right
   separately. The tattoo sleeve goes on a **separate overlay** on the tattooed arm only, so the base
   arm art stays mirrored.
2. **Mirror the bones, not just the art.** Bone lengths must match exactly (type the numbers). The shoulder
   joint must sit at the same distance from the centre line on both sides.
3. **Match the rest rotation.** A 5° difference in shoulder rotation reads as a length difference, because
   it changes how much the elbow foreshortens and how much bend shows. Rest pose: both arms 8–12° out
   from vertical, elbows bent 10–15°.
4. **Match forearm family.** A neutral forearm (0.38 H) next to a supine one (0.45 H) reads as "one arm
   thicker". In a symmetric stand both must use the same piece.
5. **Tattoo visual weight.** A dense dark sleeve makes the arm look **thinner** (dark recedes). Keep the
   tattooed arm's outline weight and skin-edge rim light equal to the other arm. Leave a thin band of plain
   skin colour along the silhouette edge so the contour position stays the same.
6. **Measure in the export**: hanging fingertips must land on the same horizontal line within 1% of H.
   The wrist creases must line up, and so must the outer deltoid points. Overlay a flipped copy at 50% opacity.
7. **Torso overlap.** If one arm overlaps the torso more (closer to the body), it looks shorter and thinner.
   Keep the gap between arm and waist the same on both sides.

## 5. Review checklist (run for every gesture frame)

**Rotation logic**
- [ ] Classified with the section 0 table: elbow-end surface vs wrist-end surface, giving supine, neutral, or prone.
- [ ] The forearm piece matches that family: no supine drawing under a pronated hand, or the reverse.
- [ ] Thumb on the correct side. Hanging arm: supine = out, neutral = forward, prone = in. Raised arm: check the table row.
- [ ] Ulna furrow and pinky-side wrist knob visible only when pronated or seen from the back.
- [ ] Brachioradialis bulge on the thumb side, high, overlapping into the upper arm.

**Elbow**
- [ ] Correct elbow face for this pose (crook vs olecranon point). The elbow points the way the table says.
- [ ] Shoulder, elbow, and wrist **not** in a straight line on any bend over 20°; there is a visible corner.
- [ ] No gap, notch or background showing at the elbow at **every keyed angle**. Scrub the timeline, don't just look at the keys.
- [ ] On bends of 90° and more, the forearm flesh presses into the biceps, the crease is one line, and the olecranon corner is drawn.
- [ ] Upper arm shows the right face (front / outer / inner) for the shoulder rotation.

**Hand**
- [ ] Hand length about 0.8 H; knuckle width about 0.38 H in front view; fingers not uniform tubes.
- [ ] Wrist break is natural (the hand bends less toward the thumb side, about 20°, than the pinky side, about 50°).
- [ ] Gesture reads as a silhouette at thumbnail size (V, heart, thumb, point).

**Proportion and symmetry**
- [ ] Elbow at about the waist and wrist at about the crotch when hanging; fingertips just below mid-thigh.
- [ ] Both arms: same bone lengths, same forearm family in symmetric poses, fingertip lines within 1% of H.
- [ ] Width rhythm present: deltoid > biceps > elbow < forearm belly > wrist.

**Tattoo sleeve**
- [ ] Pattern continuous across the elbow (no seam, no smear in the crook at this angle).
- [ ] On swapped pre-bent drawings the tattoo was redrawn to follow the fold, not pasted.
- [ ] The tattooed arm's contour is not visually thinner than the bare arm.

**Swap hygiene**
- [ ] Drawing swaps happen on a held or fast frame; shoulder and wrist pixels stay put across the swap.
